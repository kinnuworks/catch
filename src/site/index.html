Why I built it

The $300 rule was in the terms.

Nobody reads terms of service, and nobody is going to start. So the reading has to happen at the one moment it matters.

Before

Scroll, click, find out in month two.

The rule sits next to the button.

At the button

It reads where you're about to agree.

Code quotes, Gemma checks

The model can't invent a clause.

Rules pick the sentences that might be trouble. Gemma answers one yes-or-no question about each, and Catch reads its confidence instead of trusting one sampled word. The line on the card is written from numbers in the quoted sentence, so $300 can't turn into $30.

Private

Nothing you browse leaves your laptop.

An extension that reads every sign-up page you visit should never phone home. Gemma 4 E2B runs in Chrome on your own GPU through WebGPU. The weights download once from Hugging Face and stay in the browser cache. After that, Catch makes no network requests of its own.

What it looks for

Six ways terms cost you money.

Auto-renews

It keeps charging until you cancel, monthly or yearly.

Trial turns paid

The free week becomes a paid plan unless you act in time.

Lock-in

Billed monthly, but you can't leave before a minimum term ends.

Exit fee

Leaving early costs a fee, or the rest of the term.

Cancel rules

Notice periods, deadlines, or cancelling by post or phone only.

No refunds

Money you've already paid stays paid.

Accuracy

Tested on 27 real terms documents.

I fetched the terms of 50 subscription services; 27 could be read as plain text. Catch picked up to 8 suspect sentences from each, 136 in all, and I labelled every one by hand without seeing what the rules or the model said.

Questions

Things people ask.

Does Catch send my browsing anywhere?

No. The page text is read inside your browser and the model runs on your GPU. The only network traffic Catch causes is the one-time download of the model weights from Hugging Face, plus fetching a site's own terms page when the sign-up page links to it.

Why is the first run a 3.4 GB download?

That's Gemma 4 E2B with 4-bit weights. It downloads once and the browser caches it. I tried the 0.8 GB Gemma 3 1B first; it answered "yes" to nearly every question, so it couldn't tell a trap from a refund policy.

What if my computer doesn't have WebGPU?

Catch falls back to its rules. You still get the card and the exit plan; the rules flag more false alarms because nothing double-checks them.

Can the model make up a clause?

It can't add one. Every line quotes a real sentence from the page or its linked terms, and the numbers on the card come from that sentence. The model only decides whether a sentence the rules flagged really is a catch.

How does it work out the dates?

Code finds the trial length, the minimum term and the notice period in the terms, even when they're in different sentences, then does calendar arithmetic from today. It keeps a two-day safety margin before each deadline.

Is this legal advice?

No. It points you at the sentences worth reading so you can read two lines instead of twelve thousand words.

What does it miss?

Terms that only appear in images, PDFs or pop-ups that open after a click, and traps written in words the rules don't expect. The accuracy section above has the numbers.
